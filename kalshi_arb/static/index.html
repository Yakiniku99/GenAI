Mutually exclusive events. A fair set sums to $1.00. YES asks summing under $1 or YES bids summing over $1 signal arbitrage.

How opportunities are found

Buy every NO (bucket set)
In a mutually exclusive event at most one outcome resolves YES, so k NO contracts always pay at least $(k−1). It's an arbitrage when the NO asks sum to less than that. This needs only mutual exclusivity, so it's the most robust signal.
Buy every YES (bucket set)
If exactly one outcome must happen, one YES from every outcome pays exactly $1. It's an arbitrage when the YES asks sum to less than $1. This is only safe when the listed outcomes are exhaustive. That is verified automatically when the strike ranges cover the whole number line; otherwise the basket is tagged ⚠ verify exhaustive and you must read the rules (e.g. a "which quarter does the recession start" set has no "no recession" leg).
Strike-ladder inversion
For threshold ladders like "Fed rate above 3.00% / above 3.25%", a higher strike can never be more likely than a lower one. Buying YES on the lower strike and NO on the higher strike pays at least $1 in every outcome. It's an arbitrage when the higher strike's YES bid is above the lower strike's YES ask.
Costs & sizing
Prices are top-of-book asks (a NO ask is $1 − YES bid). Each leg is one order and pays the fees of the selected venue, currently —. Choose the venue when starting the server with --fees robinhood | robinhood-gold | kalshi. Fees are charged when you buy; holding to settlement adds no closing trade. Size is capped by the thinnest leg's top-level depth. Payouts arrive at settlement, so annualized return uses the latest expiration in the basket.
Liquidity
Each leg shows its bid-ask spread, contracts traded in the last 24 hours, and open interest. For the top candidates it also shows contracts offered within 5¢ of the best price. A basket's tier comes from its widest spread and the dollars you can deploy at the best prices: High ≤ 2¢ and ≥ $1,000, Medium ≤ 5¢ and ≥ $100, Low ≤ 10¢ and ≥ $10, otherwise Thin. Full-book fill reads each leg's full order book and buys basket after basket down the book while each one is still profitable after fees, which shows how much of an arbitrage is really capturable.
Trading through Robinhood
Robinhood charges a commission of k × P × (1−P) per contract, rounded up to the cent and capped at 1¢ per contract (k = 10%, or 5% with Gold). It also passes through Kalshi's exchange fee of 1¢ per contract per side. Kalshi's own taker fee doesn't apply on top. Every leg costs at least 1¢ per contract, so baskets with many cheap legs are hit hardest. Robinhood doesn't list every Kalshi market; check that each leg is tradable there before relying on a basket.
Caveats
Quotes can move between legs, books are only read at the top level, and some events settle on rules that differ from their titles. Treat this as a screen, not an execution signal. Confirm the rules and live book before trading.
